Accessible science, built together

The ACCESS Lab studies how disability is experienced across biomedical research, health care, education and STEM workplaces. We work alongside people with lived experience to build fairer systems of research, education and care.

What ACCESS stands for

Our name brings together six ideas at the centre of our work.

  • AAccessibility
  • CCitizenship
  • CCommunity
  • EEquity
  • SScience
  • SSociety

How we work

  • Lived experience

    We partner with people with lived experience of disability and chronic health conditions to understand how research, education and health care are experienced in everyday life.

  • Storytelling

    We create opportunities to share lived experience through research, education and public engagement. These stories help shape more accessible and inclusive systems.

  • Accessibility education

    We help train the next generation of accessibility leaders through graduate education focused on inclusion, accessibility and systems change.

  • Co-creation

    We bring together community members, learners, researchers, clinicians and organizations to create knowledge and drive meaningful change.

Indigenous affirmation

On unceded Algonquin territory

We pay respect to the Algonquin people, who are the traditional guardians of this land. We acknowledge their longstanding relationship with this territory, which remains unceded. We pay respect to all Indigenous people in this region, from all nations across Canada, who call Ottawa home. We acknowledge the traditional knowledge keepers, both young and old. And we honour their courageous leaders: past, present, and future.

At the ACCESS Lab, our work asks who belongs in science, health care and education, and whose knowledge counts. We recognize that Indigenous Peoples, including Indigenous people living with disability, continue to face barriers in systems shaped by colonization.

We commit to learning from Indigenous knowledge keepers and communities, and to building research relationships grounded in respect, reciprocity and accountability.

Adapted from the University of Ottawa Indigenous affirmation, which is also available in Anishinàbemowin.
